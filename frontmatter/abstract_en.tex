\frontchapter{ABSTRACT}

Three-dimensional medical image segmentation requires the coordinated organization of local anatomical details, long-range context across regions and slices, and structural information represented at different scales of a U-shaped network. Fine-grained spatial representations are essential for describing local boundaries, textures, and neighborhood continuity, whereas anatomical relationships between distant regions require information exchange over a broader spatial range. At the same time, the spatial resolution and semantic level of features change continuously during encoding and decoding, so the same structure is represented under different conditions at different scales. To address these coupled issues, this thesis investigates information coordination in volumetric medical image segmentation from two connected dimensions: local--global coordination in the spatial dimension and structural-information organization in the scale dimension.

To coordinate local spatial features with long-range context, this thesis proposes ViL-UNet, a three-dimensional medical image segmentation method based on Vision-xLSTM local--global collaborative modeling. A 3D CNN is first used to construct local and hierarchical volumetric features, after which deep features are organized as visual sequences and processed by Vision-xLSTM for long-range contextual interaction. Multi-directional sequence organization further extends information propagation along different spatial directions, allowing local convolutional representations and cross-region, cross-slice context to play complementary roles within the same framework. During decoding, spatial resolution is progressively recovered, while cross-level features are integrated to combine local details, hierarchical semantics, and deep contextual information. On the Synapse dataset, ViL-UNet achieved a mean Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) of 85.12\% and a 95th-percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95) of 12.49~mm; on the ACDC dataset, it achieved a mean DSC of 92.79\%. These results indicate favorable segmentation performance for the proposed local--global coordination framework on the two public three-dimensional medical image segmentation tasks.

After local--global relations are organized in the spatial dimension, structural information at different spatial resolutions and semantic levels within the U-shaped hierarchy still requires dedicated organization. A controlled preliminary comparison showed that persistent accumulation of cross-scale historical states did not yield directionally consistent favorable changes across different evaluation dimensions. This observation shifted the focus from increasing cross-scale propagation to maintaining correspondence between structural information and the current scale. Based on this motivation, this thesis further proposes a three-dimensional medical image segmentation method based on scale-specific structural evidence modulation. At each scale, corresponding scale-specific structural evidence is independently constructed from the current hierarchical features so that its source retains a clear scale-level correspondence. The current decoder state and the corresponding structural evidence are then jointly used to generate channel-wise adaptive modulation, linking the utilization of structural information to the current reconstruction state. The resulting structural enhancement is integrated into the decoder representation through a residual pathway, while the basic decoding pathway is retained and continues to support spatial reconstruction at the corresponding scale. Around this scale-information organization chain, an analysis framework is established from the perspectives of overall segmentation performance, organ-level differences, scale-information organization strategies, configuration relationships, and model complexity, so as to examine how structural information is formed and utilized across scales.

The two studies form a progressive research route from spatial relations to scale-level relations under the same problem of information organization in three-dimensional medical image segmentation. The first study emphasizes the functional complementarity between local structural representation and long-range context in the spatial dimension, while the second further focuses on the correspondence and utilization relationship between scale-level structural information and the current decoder state. Together, they establish a continuous technical route from local three-dimensional feature modeling, through long-range spatial context organization, to scale-level structural-information utilization. This progression provides a unified research perspective for studying how information with different spatial ranges and hierarchical roles can be coordinated within three-dimensional medical image segmentation frameworks.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} 3D medical image segmentation; Vision-xLSTM; local--global coordination; scale-level structural information; multi-scale modeling
